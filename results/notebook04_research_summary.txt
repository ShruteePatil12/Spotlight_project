SPOTLIGHT — NOTEBOOK 04 FINAL RESEARCH SUMMARY
==================================================

Research Objective
-----------------
Evaluate whether Spotlight's interest-constrained, creator-aware
recommendation intervention improves exposure opportunities for
Emerging creators while maintaining recommendation relevance.

Dataset and Evaluation
---------------------
Users evaluated: 1,411
Recommendations per user: 10
Total recommendation slots per model: 14,110

Baseline: User-based Collaborative Filtering
Intervention: Spotlight creator-aware constrained re-ranking

DIVERSITY
---------
All five evaluated diversity metrics increased:
- Unique creators per recommendation list
- Unique categories per recommendation list
- Creator diversity
- Creator pairwise diversity
- Category pairwise diversity

All five diversity improvements were statistically significant
after Holm correction.

TRADE-OFFS
-----------
Spotlight improved Emerging creator coverage, reduced zero exposure,
decreased Emerging creator exposure concentration, increased
recommendation diversity, and increased popularity-based novelty.

These gains were accompanied by small decreases in ranking relevance
and a slight deterioration in Established creator exposure metrics.

LIMITATIONS
----------
1. Emerging creators are defined by the dataset's relative popularity
   classification, not necessarily by creator age or account creation date.

2. Evaluation is based on offline interaction data and does not establish
   real-world causal impact.

3. Recommendation relevance decreased slightly under Spotlight.

4. Emerging creator fairness improved, but Established creator
   distribution was not uniformly improved.

5. Popularity-based novelty is not equivalent to personalized novelty.

6. Statistical corrections were applied within evaluation metric
   families, not globally across all evaluation families.

7. Results are specific to the evaluated dataset and experimental setup.

FINAL CONCLUSION
---------------
Spotlight demonstrated that creator-aware recommendation re-ranking
can improve Emerging creator exposure distribution, reduce creator
exposure concentration, improve diversity, and promote less-popular
content while maintaining relatively similar ranking quality.

However, these improvements involve a measurable relevance trade-off
and redistribution of exposure from Established creators.

The findings support the potential of interest-constrained creator-aware
recommendation interventions, rather than demonstrating universal
superiority over conventional collaborative filtering.
